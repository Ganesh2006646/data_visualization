⚠️ [MISLEADING] Truncated Baseline (Starts at 0.200) Bad Practice
❌ Why This Visual Misleads Policymakers

By truncating the Y-axis to start at 0.200 instead of 0.0, a modest 10% change from 0.2100 to 0.2316 pieces/m³ looks like an alarming 300% visual spike! It completely omits the Interquartile Range and hides the fact that cruise expeditions shifted during COVID-19.

✅ [ETHICAL REDESIGN] True Zero Baseline, IQR & Sample Sizes Ethical Design
✔️ Why This Visual Preserves Scientific Truth

With a true zero baseline, annual medians are virtually flat (~0.21–0.23). The Interquartile Range (Q25–Q75) spans up to 1.02 pieces/m³, demonstrating that spatial ocean variance completely dwarfs year-over-year median differences.

Multi-Compartment Distribution Across All 4 Marine Settings (Independent Scales) V5 • Unit Separation
💡 Key Ethical Principle — The "Unit Trap" Safeguard

Notice the vast scale separation: Ocean water has a median of 0.009 pieces/m³; Beach sand has a median of 4,080 pieces/m³; Nurdle Patrol reports timed counts (median 1.0 / 10 mins); and Sediment cores are measured per dry kilogram. Pooling these four compartments into a single average is mathematically and scientifically invalid.

🎓 Four Cardinal Rules for Ethical Environmental Data Visualization (CSE355)
1. Enforce Zero Baselines: Linear bar charts must always start at zero to avoid exaggerating minor fluctuations.
2. Quantify Uncertainty: Always display IQR spreads or error bars for skewed environmental distributions.
3. State Sample Sizes (n): Shifts in sample counts often drive perceived trends rather than physical changes.
4. Partition Units Strictly: Never aggregate incompatible physical metrics (volume, mass, timed walks).
